01 · Why matcha

Calm energy. A ritual a generation chose over coffee.

Matcha is the whole tea leaf, shade-grown, stone-ground and whisked into water. It went from tea house to café order to supermarket fridge in a few short years.

How drinkers describe it

A steady lift, not a spike.

Matcha carries caffeine alongside L-theanine, an amino acid that shade-grown leaf is naturally rich in. Drinkers describe a steady, focused lift without the crash they know from coffee.

The taste is part of the pull. Grassy, sweet, savoury. A colour you can see across the room. Whisking a bowl is a small daily ritual people choose to keep.

Whole leaf

You drink the leaf, so quality matters.

Most tea is steeped and the leaf thrown away. Matcha is not. The leaf is ground and drunk whole, so everything about how it was grown ends up in the cup.

The top grades come from young spring leaves, shaded for weeks before picking, with stems and veins removed before milling. Lower grades go into lattes, baking and bottled drinks. Buyers pay for origin, grade and clean cultivation.

The shift

From niche café order to supermarket fridge.

Matcha moved from specialist tea houses to coffee chains, then into cans, ice cream and snack aisles. Japan's green tea exports more than doubled in value in a single year, driven by powdered tea.

Outside the big cities, awareness is still low. That is headroom, not a ceiling.
