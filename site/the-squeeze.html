02 · The squeeze

Demand climbs. Constrained Supply.

Matcha demand is global. Tencha supply comes from a few regions of Japan, one harvest a year, and a shrinking pool of land and farmers. Prices show it.

  1. i

    Tencha is under 10% of Japan's tea crop.

    Japan made about 6,278 tonnes of tencha in 2025, 8.6% of its crude tea output. Output has roughly tripled since 2014 and still trails demand.3

  2. ii

    Tea land is shrinking.

    Japan's tea fields covered 31,900 ha in 2026, down from 42,400 ha in 2017. A quarter of the land gone in under a decade, and it falls every year.4

  3. iii

    Farmers are ageing.

    Japan's core farm workforce fell 25% between 2020 and 2025, to about 1.02 million, with an average age of 67.6. These are all-farm figures. Tea relies on the same workforce.5

  4. iv

    One spring harvest carries the whole year.

    Matcha leaf is picked once a year, in late spring. The volume for the next twelve months is fixed before anyone knows demand. A frost or a heatwave hits the whole year.6

  5. v

    Shops in Japan cap sales at one tin per customer.

    Specialty stores in Tokyo and Kyoto have limited buyers to one item per person. Leading Kyoto houses have cut ranges and raised prices to protect core blends.6, 7

What it means

Buyers outside Japan are rationed first.

Prices are rising at every step, from auction to café. Export buyers sit at the back of the queue.

A clean, year-round source close to European customers is worth paying for.
